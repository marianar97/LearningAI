#### Goal: 
Understand the customer problem with enough precision to scope a solution

Customers normally describe symptoms (e.g: our team is spending a lot of hours), a solution they want (e.g: a chatbot ui), or an outcome (e.g: we want AI to make our life easier). You should work backwards to **identify the real problem** 


#### Who is in the discovery call:
The discovery call should be three types of people:
- **Executive sponsor**: control the budget, and anchors the conversation to what the organization priorities, what they will fund and act on.
- **Ops person or domain expert:** performs the day to day work. They describe the system they work on
- **Technical**: knows which systems are involved (API, DB) and how to access them. They inform on the feasibility of the problem

#### Discovery session
1. **Problem understanding** what is the failure in the current process: what triggers the failure, who gets involved, what work gets stuck, and how the team recovers. Pay special attention to: 
	Data systems involved
	- time (latency) and frequency (how often)
	- ownership gap (who own each step in the system)
2. **Confirm and Verify**: 
	- Summarize what you understand. Including:
		- core problem 
		- systems involved: e
		- success criteria
	- Checklist: Before leaving the session
	    - [ ] Does the data required for the system exist, and is it accessible within the project timeline?
	    - [ ] Is there a specific, measurable success criterion that can be verified before the project ends?
	    - [ ] Is the customer’s timeline realistic given what actually needs to be built?
	    - [ ] Is there one person inside the organization who owns the problem, has authority to clear blockers, and will be the main contact throughout?

**Do not:** 
- Offer a solution in a discovery call
- Focus only on the senior roles opinion. If seniors roles dominate the conversation, redirect it to people who do the day to day work
- Scope creep: close the session with was identified, not an invitation for additions.
- skipping the baseline: scoping a solution without knowing the current process costs in time, error, rate make it impossible to build a business case
